Selection in maize: Tajima's D and PBS from genotype likelihoods¶

Purpose. Domestication is a selection experiment run by people over thousands of years, and maize is the textbook case. This exercise looks for its signature two ways: a shift in the site frequency spectrum measured by Tajima's D, and population differentiation measured by PBS. Everything is computed with ANGSD from genotype likelihoods, never from called genotypes, because the sequencing is low depth.

What you will do

  • estimate the SFS for a maize population with realSFS
  • compute Tajima's D in sliding windows with thetaStat and plot it along chromosome 4
  • see how much the window size changes the picture
  • compute PBS for a highland population against lowland relatives, to separate local adaptation from the domestication signal shared by all maize

The data. Maize and its wild relatives (Zea mays), 50 low-depth BAMs:

Group What it is n
RIMMA domesticated maize 15
Tularosa (SW750) highland maize, US southwest 20
Ozarks maize from the Ozark highlands 18
TIL* parviglumis, the wild ancestor ~17

The genotypes are not called — the exercise works from site allele frequency likelihoods (SAF). At this depth calling a genotype would bias the SFS exactly where Tajima's D reads it, which is the reason for the whole approach.

Two reference genomes are supplied: B73 (the domesticated maize reference) and TDD39103 (a parviglumis assembly).

Note. The ANGSD steps that produce the SAF files take 15+ minutes, so they are shown but left commented out, with the results provided.

Before this do SFS, Fst and PBS, which introduces the same statistics on human data.

Population Branch Statistic (PBS)¶

Now we will look for selection specificially in a population of ancient maize in Eastern North America (ENA). We would like to know if there has been any selection in the evolutionary lineage that gave rise to maize in ENA. We know that maize dispersed north from the domestication center reaching the US Southwest (US SW) ~4000 years ago, and then moved into ENA between 2000-1000 years ago (Figure below,left side).

So, for this exercise we will use genomic data from three populations:

  • 9 genomes from the Ozarks rockshelters in ENA
  • 10 genomes from the Tularosa cave in the US SW
  • 16 genomes from maize's wild ancestor Zea mays subsp. parviglumis So, given how these populations are related, we want to setup a PBS test like the one in the figure below (left side)
